# Hulk Estate content playbook

## The job of a property post

A property post is not a brochure. It has one job: make a qualified buyer send a WhatsApp
message. Everything in the post either moves toward that or gets cut.

## What makes property posts convert

1. **A number in the first three words.** Price, instalment, yield, or the gap to market. It is
   the only thing that stops the scroll on a listing post.
2. **One reason this listing is different.** That's the `Highlight` column — corner lot, under
   market, 4 minutes to transit. If a row has no highlight, the post has no hook; fill the
   column before posting it.
3. **Specs as a scannable list, not a sentence.** Nobody reads "this beautiful three-bedroom
   home features". They read `3R2B · 1,050 sqft · Freehold`.
4. **A CTA with a prefilled message.** The wa.me link opens WhatsApp with the property reference
   already typed. Removing friction at that exact step is most of the conversion.
5. **Honest omission over padding.** A missing field is dropped, never guessed and never
   softened into "price on request, but a steal".

## Framework selection

| Row looks like | Use |
|---|---|
| Strong price story, `Highlight` is about value | `price_anchor` |
| Has `rental_yield` or `monthly_instalment`, investor-facing | `yield_math` |
| `Highlight` is transit/access/surroundings | `location_first` |
| Solid listing, no single standout angle | `quick_spec_drop` |

## Cadence

Two posts a day, different properties (the cooldown enforces this). Morning post skews
investor (`yield_math`, `price_anchor`); evening post skews lifestyle/occupier
(`location_first`, `quick_spec_drop`).

## Reply discipline

The bot's reply is a door-opener, not a negotiation. It answers the literal question with one
fact from the sheet, then moves the conversation to WhatsApp where you can qualify properly.
Never let a reply template quote terms, discounts, or loan approval odds — those are
conversations, not templates.
