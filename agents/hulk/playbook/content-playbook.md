# Hulk Content Playbook

Distilled from `creator-research.md` (pass 1, 6 creators) and `creator-research-pass2.md` (pass
2, 11 more creators — 17 total). This is the actionable layer — use it to pick a hook and a
framework for each post. See `../templates/` for fill-in-the-blank versions of each framework.

Scope reminder: finance, trading, stock market, and cryptocurrency **only**. See
`../config/topics.yaml` for the explicit allow/deny list.

## Hook patterns

| Hook | What it does | Source | Example shape |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Numbers-first** | Leads with a real, specific number instead of a vague claim | ringgitsidehustle, stocktwits | "Portfolio: RM258.9k crypto, RM253.3k EPF, RM131.7k stocks, RM35.1k ASNB." |
| **Conditional reframe** | "If you want [outcome], [do counterintuitive thing]" | hormozi | "If you're still averaging down on a loser, you're not managing risk — you're gambling." |
| **Contrarian reframe** | Subverts the expected answer / goes against crowd sentiment | hormozi, cryptokaleo | "Your biggest risk right now isn't the drawdown, it's how confident you are it won't happen." |
| **Confession → lesson** | Admits a loss/mistake before pivoting to advice — reads as more credible than a highlight reel | ringgitsidehustle | "Lost 100% of a RM67k crypto position in 2022. Here's the one rule that would've saved it." |
| **Curiosity gap + directional emoji** | Names a mover/event, teases the reason, arrow points down to it | stocktwits | "BTC dominance just did something it hasn't done since 2021 👇" |
| **Position/"bags" disclosure** | States a real or illustrative position as the opening line | cryptokaleo | "Bought the dip on [asset]. Here's why." |
| **Standalone aphorism** | One complete, quotable thought, no setup, no elaboration | hormozi | "The trade you didn't take is still a decision." |
| **False-binary rejection** | Stage two opposing consensus camps, reject both, present a third data-backed position | bitcoin.daily | "Everyone's either screaming 'the bottom is in' or 'it's dead.' Both are wrong. Here's what the data says." |
| **Ritual/cadence hook** | Opens by naming a recurring content ritual, trading on habit/anticipation rather than a one-off payload | hyperstocks | "Our favorite time of the week — the watchlist 👇" |
| **Audience question / poll** | Pure engagement-bait question, offers no information, asks the audience to supply the content | investments | "What 'hype' trade are you avoiding right now?" |
| **Historical-proof reveal** | Walks a real, dated historical chronology (splits, cycles, price history) to a jaw-dropping terminal number — credibility from documented fact, not personal risk | 13finance | "₹100 became ₹14.1 crore. Here's the exact 44-year timeline." |

## Content frameworks

1. **Progress-reveal / journal framework** — chronological list of a metric (portfolio value,
   win rate, account growth) followed by a short lesson. High-volume, screenshot-friendly.
2. **Call → reasoning → risk caveat** — state a market view, one line of technical/sentiment
   reasoning, optional risk-management caveat. The only framework here purpose-built for
   finance/crypto rather than adapted from general business content — use for actual market
   takes.
3. **Standalone aphorism/principle** — one evergreen trading/finance principle, no elaboration.
   Low production cost, low risk (no specific prediction to be wrong about), good for daily
   cadence.
4. **Listicle/bulleted breakdown** — scannable list (trending tickers, portfolio allocation,
   numbered rules/mistakes). The most format-agnostic pattern across every creator studied.
5. **Confession → lesson** — real or illustrative loss/mistake, then the takeaway. Use sparingly
   so it retains credibility weight.
6. **Historical-proof compounding reveal** — a subtype of progress-reveal built on real, public
   historical data (a stock's split/bonus history, a coin's cycle history) rather than the
   poster's own trading. No personal risk disclosure needed since it's factual/historical.
7. **Track-record / proof-of-performance** — self vs. benchmark comparison stated as a hard
   number ("+30.7% vs. the S&P 500's +16.4%"). Use only with real, verifiable figures — never
   fabricate a performance claim.

## Tone/voice device: short-declarative closing rhythm

Not a hook or a framework, but a recurring stylistic device worth using deliberately: close a
post with two or three short, punchy declarative sentences ("Both are wrong." / "Small risk
feels boring... Until boring becomes freedom."). This is what makes a post quotable and
screenshot-able — reserve it for the last line, not the whole post.

## Crypto-specific: anti-impersonation trust signal

Crypto creators specifically (not stock-market creators) commonly include a bio-level or
periodic-post disclaimer against impersonation scams, e.g. "I will never DM you first or ask you
to send money/crypto." This is worth adopting for Hulk's own bio/pinned content once Hulk has a
real account and following, purely as a defensive/trust measure against impersonators — not as a
content hook to imitate in regular posts.

## Topic pillars

- Stock market: earnings reactions, sector moves, technical setups, macro data (CPI, rate
  decisions) and their market impact.
- Trading: technical/fundamental analysis, trade setups, risk management, trading psychology.
- Crypto: BTC/ETH majors, altcoin cycles, on-chain signals, market structure, cycle timing.
- Personal finance (trading-adjacent only): portfolio allocation, net worth tracking as it
  relates to investing — not general budgeting/lifestyle content.

## CTA styles (in order of preference)

1. **None.** Default. Let the value of the post carry engagement — this is the dominant pattern
   across the best-performing creators studied (hormozi, ringgitsidehustle).
2. **Native question** ("What's your read on this?" / "Anyone else in this position?") — light,
   conversational, no ask beyond a reply.
3. **Directional link-out** ("👇 [link]") — only when there's a genuine reason to send traffic
   somewhere (e.g. a linked chart, a longer analysis). Avoid if there's nothing to link to.
4. Never a hard sales pitch, course plug, or signal-group pitch — Hulk is not selling anything.

## Strategic notes from pass-2 research

- **Threads is a secondary channel for most large finance creators.** Several accounts with huge
  X/TikTok followings had surprisingly small Threads-specific followings (one account: 2.7K on
  Threads vs. 1.2M on X). Most competitors are cross-posting, not writing Threads-native content
  — a genuinely Threads-native strategy has real room to outperform on this platform specifically.
- **Broad financial-literacy framing outperformed narrow trade-call content** in the accounts
  studied — the largest Threads-specific followings belonged to accounts explaining concepts
  (rules of thumb, economic literacy) rather than making individual stock/coin calls. Worth
  weighting the content mix toward educational/explainer posts, not just calls.
- **Named, memorable "rules"** (e.g. a "35% rule" for rent affordability) paired with a real,
  personally-anchored number are a strong, repeatable hook mechanism — consider giving Hulk's own
  recurring principles memorable names.

## Production principle (from Hormozi's pipeline)

Where possible, validate an idea in its cheapest form (a single short post) before investing in
anything longer. Don't build out a multi-post chain or elaborate format until a simpler version
of the same idea has shown it resonates.

## Performance review — 2026-08-10

First real-data checkpoint, 32 posts across days 739827–739837 (2026-07-30 through 2026-08-09).
Trigger: the operator noticed total daily views looked like they were declining and asked for a
full analysis, competitive research, and a posting-cadence sanity check.

**Finding 1 — it isn't a time-based decline.** `corr(day_index, daily_total_views) = -0.29`
(weak). What actually explains the volatility: `corr(day_has_historical_compounding_reveal,
daily_total_views) = 0.90` (strong). The framework mix, not the calendar, drives the swings —
days 08-03/08-04/08-06/08-07 (the visibly "low" days) simply didn't roll a
`historical_compounding_reveal` slot in the deterministic rotation that week.

**Finding 2 — per-framework split (avg views / n, latest snapshot per post):**

| Framework | n | avg views | likes/post | replies/post | note |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| historical_compounding_reveal | 3 | 262 | 0.00 | 0.00 | huge reach, **zero** interaction across all 3 |
| contrarian_reframe | 3 | 81 | 0.67 | 0.33 | best reply rate among high-view frameworks |
| call_reasoning_risk | 4 | 75 | 0.00 | 0.25 | |
| STRIKER (Striker Zones slot) | 4 | 65 | 0.50 | 0.00 | |
| standalone_aphorism | 4 | 42 | 0.75 | 0.00 | |
| progress_reveal | 4 | 39 | 1.00 | 0.00 | **best engagement rate** (3.2%) despite modest reach |
| listicle_breakdown | 4 | 38 | 0.50 | 0.00 | |
| audience_question | 4 | 38 | 0.25 | 0.00 | underperforming its own job — designed to prompt replies, isn't |
| confession_lesson | 2 | 24 | 0.50 | 0.00 | weakest, but smallest sample (n=2) |

The reach leader and the engagement-rate leader are different frameworks — optimizing for either
one alone would be a mistake. `historical_compounding_reveal` posts get seen by far more people
but generate no likes/replies at all; `progress_reveal` converts a much smaller audience into
actual interaction at 4x the rate. Both matter (reach compounds distribution over time; replies
are weighted more heavily than likes by Threads' own ranking, per the research below).

**Finding 3 — external research (Aug 2026), used to sanity-check rather than replace the above:**
- Threads: 2-3 posts/day is the accepted range for consistent growth; dilution (posts competing
  with each other for the same audience) sets in past roughly 5/day. **Hulk's 3x/day is not
  over-posting** by current benchmarks — the operator's instinct to cut volume isn't supported by
  either the internal data (weak time correlation) or external benchmarks.
- Replies outrank likes in Threads' distribution algorithm — "10 thoughtful replies distributes
  wider than 100 likes and zero conversation." Reinforces weighting toward
  reply-generating frameworks (contrarian_reframe, progress_reveal-style), not purely toward raw
  view count.
- Visual posts get ~3x the engagement of text-only. Hulk already attaches a poster to 31/32 posts
  in this sample (the account's own near-100% coverage already matches this finding) — see the
  persona voice-rule update making this explicit/mandatory rather than "selective" language that
  had drifted from what the code actually does.

**Decision (implemented same day, see `scripts/daily_post.py`):** replaced the flat 8-way
round-robin with a weighted, hand-interleaved `ROTATION` — `historical_compounding_reveal` ×3,
`contrarian_reframe`/`call_reasoning_risk`/`progress_reveal` ×2 each, the remaining four frameworks
×1 each (13-slot cycle, no framework repeats back-to-back including across the wrap). This leans
into the reach leader without abandoning the frameworks that actually build interaction — a
"balanced portfolio" call, not a "biggest number wins" one.

**Not changed:** posting cadence (3x/day) — external and internal data both say this is fine.
`audience_question` and `confession_lesson` stay in rotation at low weight rather than being cut —
sample sizes (n=4, n=2) are too small to retire a format on, and `audience_question` in particular
is worth watching rather than discarding, since it's underperforming at the one thing (replies)
it's specifically designed to do — that reads as a content-execution problem (is the model asking
a genuinely reply-worthy question?), not a proof the format itself doesn't work.

**Next review:** see the weekly check-in Routine — re-pull this same breakdown once the reweighted
rotation has enough new data (aim for 2+ more instances of each framework), compare per-framework
averages against this baseline, and adjust `FRAMEWORK_WEIGHTS` again rather than treating this as
a one-time fix.
