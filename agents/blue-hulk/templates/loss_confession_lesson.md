# Template: Loss-First Confession → Lesson (flagship)

Use when: telling a loss/mistake story that resolves into a discipline lesson. Blue Hulk's
strongest framework — vulnerability reframed as authority.
Emotion: regret or loss aversion. CTA: engagement question or one-word trigger.

```
[Hook — the loss/exposure moment, ultra-specific: a time, a sound, a number. One short sentence.]

[The setup — 2-3 sentences of what led there. Include the uncomfortable detail everyone
recognizes but nobody posts: the deleted alert, the moved stop, the "one more entry."]

[Normalize — one line: losses are part of this. No self-flagellation, no drama.]

[The lesson — the ONE concrete behavioral change that followed. A rule, stated plainly.
Not a moral. A rule.]

[CTA — question tied to THIS story, at the emotional peak. One line. Then stop.]
```

Example shape:
```
I still remember the sound my phone made at 3:47am.

Not the alert I'd set at my stop level — I'd deleted that one two days earlier. The margin
call. And here's the part nobody posts about: I wasn't even surprised.

Losses are tuition in this business. But tuition should buy a lesson.

Mine: the stop I set before entry is placed by the version of me that's still sane. Every
version that touches it afterwards is compromised. So it doesn't get touched.

What's the trade you knew was wrong while you were still in it? I read every comment.
```

Notes:
- Never fabricate a specific personal loss presented as real (persona rule 2). Use real
  operator-supplied history, clearly illustrative framing, or switch to the third-party
  war-story template.
- One sensory/mundane detail, varied sentence rhythm, no tidy moral bow (human-touch rules).
- Passes through the pre-publish checklist (`../playbook/copywriting-engine.md` §7).
