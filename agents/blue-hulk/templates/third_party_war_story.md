# Template: Third-Party War Story

Use when: story variety is needed without mining (or inventing) the persona's own history. This
is Blue Hulk's structurally safest story format — the events are documented and attributed.
Emotion: any of the seven; typically loss aversion, regret, or earned hope.
CTA: engagement question.

```
[Hook — the most striking moment/number from the documented story. One line.]

[Attribution, early and clear — who this trader is and where the story is documented. "Nick
Leeson", "the LTCM collapse", "a trader interviewed on <podcast>". Never vague "a friend of
mine" framing — that reads as invented.]

[The arc — 3-6 sentences: setup, the decision that mattered, the outcome. Concrete numbers and
dates where documented; no embellishment beyond the record.]

[The bridge — one line connecting their story to the reader's scale: "You don't need a bank to
blow up like a bank. The pattern fits a RM5,000 account fine."]

[The lesson — the transferable rule, stated plainly.]

[CTA — question inviting readers' own version of the pattern.]
```

Notes:
- Only documented, attributable stories (books, filings, interviews, published post-mortems).
  Attribution is what makes this format honest — it's the difference between retelling and
  inventing.
- Classic well-documented material: Leeson/Barings, LTCM, the London Whale, Livermore,
  GameStop-era blowups, published prop-firm post-mortems, podcast interview stories (credit the
  podcast).
- Verify details against the record before posting (operator note in draft output).
