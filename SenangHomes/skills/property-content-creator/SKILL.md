# Senang Homes Property Content Creator

This skill helps you produce short-form video content (Reels, TikTok, Shorts) for **Senang Homes** — a Kuala Lumpur real estate brand — aimed at a niche audience of **first-time home buyers, young professionals, and condo hunters in the Klang Valley**, with the secondary goal of converting viewers into **booked viewings and WhatsApp enquiries**.

The skill is opinionated. Property content on Malaysian FinTok/PropertyTok is crowded and mostly generic ("5 tips for buying your first home!"). You stand out with **strong hooks, a clear point of view, one takeaway per video, and engagement-bait the algorithm rewards.** This skill encodes the patterns that actually work in this niche.

## When to use this skill

Use this skill whenever the user wants any of:
- A short-form video script (15–90 sec)
- A hook for a video they're about to film
- A caption with SEO hashtags
- A weekly content calendar
- A property/scheme explainer they can film on their phone
- A listing / unit showcase video
- An area guide ("what RM450k gets you in Cheras")
- A lead-gen / "book a viewing" video
- A series concept (e.g., "First Home Fridays")

Don't use this skill for: long-form YouTube tours, written blog posts, or paid ad copy — those have different mechanics.

## Core philosophy: why most property content fails

Before writing anything, internalise these three things, because they shape every decision:

1. **The first 2 seconds decide everything.** The Reels/FYP feed is ruthless. If your hook doesn't stop the scroll, nothing else matters. "Hi guys, today I want to talk about buying your first home" is a death sentence. Open with tension, a number (a price, a monthly repayment), a contradiction, or a pattern interrupt.

2. **Comments > saves > likes > follows.** The algorithm prioritises content that triggers comment-debate and saves. Property is a *high-save* niche — a "what RM500k buys in 5 KL areas" video gets saved because people want it later, and saves are gold. Engineer for it: give a takeaway worth saving, and end on a question that splits opinion ("Condo or landed for your first home? Fight it out below").

3. **Trust beats production.** This audience is making the biggest purchase of their life and is terrified of being scammed or overselling themselves. Raw, honest, face-to-camera content with a real opinion outperforms glossy developer-ad-style content with no point of view. Don't over-script. Leave room for the user's natural voice — including **Manglish / Bahasa** where that's how the audience actually talks ("Ni betul-betul worth it or not?", "Jom I show you").

## The script formula

Every short-form video follows this structure. Use it unless you have a strong reason not to.

```
[0–3s]   HOOK              — stop the scroll
[3–10s]  CONTEXT/SETUP     — why this matters now / to you
[10–45s] PAYOFF/VALUE      — the actual content (one takeaway)
[45–55s] ENGAGEMENT TRIGGER— question, hot take, or "am I wrong?"
[55–60s] SOFT CTA          — save / follow / DM to view (only if natural)
```

Notes on each section:

- **Hook**: The most important line. Spend disproportionate effort here. See `references/hooks-library.md` for proven patterns.
- **Context**: Two short sentences max. "This condo is RM420k in Old Klang Road. Here's why that's either a steal or a trap."
- **Payoff**: Where you deliver value. For property videos, give **one** specific thing — one area, one scheme, one number, one unit — not five. People remember one thing from a 60-second video. (Exception: "list" videos like "5 areas under RM500k" are saved *because* they're a list — that's a deliberate format, not the default.)
- **Engagement trigger**: Don't skip this. This is where comments come from. Ask a question that splits opinion. ("Would you buy a leasehold condo to save RM80k? Genuinely asking.")
- **CTA**: Be subtle. Aggressive "DM ME NOW!!" is cringe and reads as a hard-sell agent. "If you want the full unit breakdown, it's a DM away — no pressure, no spam" works better.

## How to write a script (the workflow)

When the user asks for a video script:

1. **Clarify if needed**, but don't over-clarify. The minimum you need: (a) topic / angle (an area? a scheme? a specific listing? a mindset piece?), (b) goal (follows, saves, comments, viewings/enquiries, authority), (c) approximate length. If the user already gave these, just write.

2. **Brainstorm 3 hooks before settling on one.** Read `references/hooks-library.md`, pick the archetype that fits, write three variations, choose the strongest. The hook is too important to one-shot.

3. **Write the script in spoken voice.** Use contractions. Use fragments. Use the way the user actually talks — including natural Manglish/Bahasa switches where the audience speaks that way. Avoid "furthermore," "moreover," "in conclusion." If a line wouldn't sound natural said out loud in KL, rewrite it.

4. **Mark timing and on-screen text.** For each section, indicate roughly how long it takes and what appears on screen. Most viewers watch sound-off — on-screen text (the price, the area, the monthly repayment) is not optional. For property, **numbers on screen** are the single highest-impact overlay.

5. **Write the caption separately.** See `references/seo-keywords.md` for hashtag strategy. The caption should add a layer the video didn't — a question, a stat, the exact price — not just summarise.

6. **Add B-roll / visual notes.** Suggest what to show: unit walkthrough clips, the view from the balcony, the facilities, a Google Maps zoom to transit, a loan-repayment calculation on screen, face-to-camera. The user films on their phone, so suggestions must be realistic and shoot-on-site friendly.

Format every script deliverable like this. It makes filming easier:

```

────────────────────

────────────────────

[0–3s]  HOOK
SAY: "..."
SHOW: [face to camera / unit / view / map / etc.]

[3–10s] SETUP
SAY: "..."
SHOW: ...
TEXT OVERLAY: "..."

... (continue for each beat)

────────────────────
CAPTION
────────────────────
[1–2 lines that add to the video, not summarise it — include the price/area keyword]

[hashtags — see hashtag block]

────────────────────
HASHTAGS
────────────────────
[15–25 hashtags, mixing tiers — see references/seo-keywords.md]

────────────────────
PIN COMMENT
────────────────────
[1 line you post as the first comment to seed engagement]
```

The pin comment is a small but powerful trick — it gives early viewers a comment to react to, which kicks off the engagement loop. For property, a great pin comment is a question that surfaces buyer intent ("What's your budget for your first place — RM300k, 400k, or 500k+?").

## Posting strategy

See `references/posting-strategy.md` for cadence, time-of-day, and series-building guidance. Short version: **4–6 posts per week, consistent timing, batch-film once per week.** Consistency outperforms polish. A 7/10 Reel posted 5x a week beats a cinematic listing tour posted once a month.

## Important: things to avoid

- **Don't make financial or price-growth guarantees.** "This will double in 5 years" is bad for compliance and credibility. "This area has [specific driver — new MRT station opening] which historically supports demand" is fine.
- **Don't quote scheme rules from memory.** Stamp-duty thresholds, SRP limits, and RUMAWIP eligibility change with every Budget. If a video hinges on a scheme figure, verify it (use `property-market-analyst` or a quick search) before scripting the number.
- **Don't oversell / pressure.** "Only 2 units left, buy now or regret forever!!" is the exact FOMO-agent energy this audience distrusts. Honesty converts better here.
- **Don't use developer-ad language.** "Nestled in a vibrant enclave of luxury living" is death. Talk like a friend who knows property, not a brochure.
- **Don't write essays.** If a script runs over 220 words for 60 seconds, cut it. People talk faster than they think, but only a little.
- **Don't lead with the listing pitch.** The whole video should earn the right to mention the unit/viewing at the end, if at all.
- **Don't post real client/owner details.** Unit numbers, exact addresses, or an owner's situation shouldn't go on a public Reel without consent.

## Working with the user

The user runs Senang Homes — a KL real estate brand for first-time buyers. They know the KL market, the areas, the schemes, and the buyer's anxieties first-hand. Your job isn't to teach them property, it's to package what they already know into formats that travel on short-form video. Lean on their real opinions and real listings; don't water them down or generic-ify them. The brand promise is making buying feel *senang* (easy) — every piece of content should reduce a first-time buyer's confusion or fear by exactly one notch. Warmth + a hook + one clear takeaway is the whole game.
