# Posting Strategy — KL Property Content

How often, when, and in what shape to post — for maximum reach and audience growth in the KL first-time-buyer niche.

## Cadence: consistency beats polish

The single highest-leverage decision: **post on a fixed schedule.** A 7/10 Reel posted 5x a week beats a cinematic listing tour posted once a month, every single time. The algorithm rewards predictable output because it can train the recommendation engine on your account.

**Recommended schedule:**
- **Instagram Reels:** 1 post per day, 5–6 days per week (Reels is the primary channel for a KL property brand — this is where Senang Homes' audience is)
- **TikTok:** Cross-post the same content with a 2-hour offset (don't post identical files at the same minute — platforms detect and de-rank), slightly rawer edit
- **YouTube Shorts:** 2–3x per week, lighter cadence, more declarative hooks

Instagram is the anchor for this brand because Malaysian property buyers, agents, and developer pages all cluster there and it doubles as a shopfront (bio link, DMs, Highlights of past listings/areas).

## Time of day (KL / Malaysia-specific)

The audience is working adults in the Klang Valley (MYT, UTC+8). Post when they're commuting, on lunch, or winding down — that's when they're on their phones thinking about their future home.

**Best windows (MYT / UTC+8):**
- **Morning commute (07:30–08:30):** LRT/MRT scrollers, coffee-in-hand
- **Lunch (12:30–13:30):** the strongest engagement window for property in KL
- **Evening (20:00–22:30):** after dinner, the "let's look at property together" couple hours — highest **save** and **DM** rates

If picking one slot per day, **evening (20:00–22:00 MYT) is the highest-intent window** — that's when couples and individuals seriously browse and enquire. Weekends (Sat/Sun late morning) are strong for area-guide and viewing content because that's when people actually go for viewings.

## The series strategy

Series content is the single biggest unlock. It does three things:
1. Trains the algorithm to push to the same audience repeatedly
2. Gives viewers a reason to follow (they're committing to a recurring format)
3. Forces you to batch-film, which is more efficient

**Proven series formats for KL property:**

- **"First Home Fridays"** — weekly, one first-time-buyer question answered (schemes, loans, S&P, mistakes)
- **"What RM___ Buys"** — the budget challenge; a price point in a KL area each week (huge saves)
- **"Area in 60 Seconds"** — one KL neighbourhood: prices, transit, honest pros/cons
- **"Scheme Explained"** — one government scheme per video (stamp duty exemption, SRP, RUMAWIP, PR1MA), verified figures on screen
- **"Rent vs Buy"** — recurring maths on a real unit vs its rental
- **"Honest Unit Tour"** — a real listing with one real drawback shown
- **"Loan Truths"** — one financing myth or fact per week (margin, DSR, why loans get rejected)

Pick **2–3 series** to run simultaneously. Don't try to run 7 — you'll burn out and miss your schedule, which is worse than running fewer. A strong pairing: one value series (First Home Fridays / Scheme Explained) + one save-magnet series (What RM___ Buys / Area in 60 Seconds) + occasional Honest Unit Tours for lead-gen.

## Batch filming

Film **5–7 videos in one session, once per week.** Vary shirts/backgrounds so they don't look filmed back-to-back. This is the single biggest production hack for holding cadence without losing your sanity.

**Batching workflow:**
1. Sunday: write the week's scripts (use this skill) — pull any scheme figures fresh via `property-market-analyst`
2. Sunday/Monday: film face-to-camera pieces in one 60–90 min session; capture listing/area B-roll opportunistically during real viewings and site visits during the week
3. Monday: edit/caption the first 2–3
4. Midweek: edit the rest
5. Schedule everything in Instagram's native scheduler / a tool like Buffer

## The "post + engage" loop

Posting alone is half the job. The other half is **engaging in the first 30–60 minutes** after you post.

Within 30 minutes of posting:
1. **Pin a comment** that triggers debate or surfaces buyer intent (see SKILL.md and lead-gen-patterns.md).
2. **Reply to every comment.** Every one. The algorithm reads this as "creator is active on this video" and pushes harder. In property, many comments are real buyer questions — answering them publicly is both engagement fuel *and* free lead qualification.
3. **Turn great comment questions into the next video** ("Someone asked in my last Reel whether leasehold kills resale — here's the honest answer").

If you can only do one thing post-publish: **reply to comments aggressively in the first hour**, and treat buyer questions as warm leads.

## Cross-platform considerations

The same video can run on Reels, TikTok, and Shorts, but with platform-specific tweaks:

- **Instagram Reels:** The primary channel. Slightly more polished, hashtag + keyword-rich caption, on-screen numbers, use Highlights to archive area guides and past listings as an evergreen shopfront.
- **TikTok:** Rawer, faster, more contrarian tone, trending local sounds, heavier text-overlay.
- **YouTube Shorts:** Lead with a more declarative hook, longer caption doubling as SEO ("RM450k condo Old Klang Road first time buyer Malaysia").

**Don't post the identical export to all three.** Re-export with platform aspect ratios and re-caption. The few extra minutes per platform pays off in reach.

## What to do when a video flops

A flop is normal. Most property content gets modest views even on established accounts. Don't react emotionally:

1. **Don't delete it.** Sometimes videos get re-tested by the algorithm weeks later. Deletion kills that chance.
2. **Don't change strategy after one flop.** Look at the rolling 14-day average — that's the signal.
3. **Do study it.** Was the hook weak? Was there a number on screen in the first 3 seconds? Was the area too niche or the topic too broad?

## What to do when a video pops

When something over-performs (a "What RM500k buys" or a scheme explainer often will):

1. **Reply to as many comments as humanly possible** — and DM the ones that show real buying intent.
2. **Within 24 hours, post a follow-up** that references it ("You all went crazy over the RM450k Cheras video — here are 3 more at that budget").
3. **Pin the popped video to your profile** and save the area/scheme to a Highlight — new profile visitors convert to follows faster.
4. **Don't change your style.** Pops are partly luck. Keep posting your normal content — the audience followed for *you* and the brand, not the gimmick.
