# Hooks Library — KL Property / First-Time Buyer Niche

The first 2–3 seconds of every video. Get this right and the rest matters; get it wrong and nothing else matters.

These are organised by archetype. When writing a script, pick the archetype that matches the angle, then write 3 variations and choose the sharpest one. Where the audience speaks Manglish/Bahasa, a mixed-language hook often stops the scroll harder than clean English — use it where it's natural.

## 1. Specific Number Hook (highest authority + saves)

A specific price, monthly repayment, or square-footage creates instant credibility and curiosity. This is the **default** for property — numbers are the whole game.

**Patterns:**
- "RM420,000 for a condo in KL. Here's the catch nobody mentions."
- "Your RM3,000 salary can buy a home in KL. Let me show you the maths."
- "This unit is RM1,850 a month. Cheaper than the rent next door. Here's how."
- "5 KL areas where RM450k still buys you a real condo in 2026."
- "I found a RM380k condo 6 minutes from an MRT. Too good to be true?"

When to use: almost always. Property buyers run on numbers. Don't fake them — put a real price on screen.

## 2. Contrarian Hook (highest comment rate)

You take a position the audience expects to disagree with. The disagreement is the comment fuel.

**Patterns:**
- "'Stop renting, buy a house' is terrible advice for most of you. I sell property."
- "Leasehold isn't the dealbreaker everyone screams about. Let me explain."
- "Buying a brand-new launch might be the worst move for a first-time buyer."
- "Everyone chasing KLCC. The smart first-home money is going somewhere boring."
- "You don't need RM50k saved to buy your first home in Malaysia. Really."

When to use: when you have a real, defensible opinion that pushes against the KL property consensus.

## 3. The Fear/Mistake Hook (high relevance)

First-time buyers are scared of one thing: making an expensive mistake. Name the fear.

**Patterns:**
- "The RM15,000 mistake most first-time buyers in KL make and never see coming."
- "I've seen people lose their booking fee over this one clause. Don't be them."
- "Before you sign that S&P, check this one thing. It's cost people their deposit."
- "Your loan got rejected and you don't know why. Here are the 3 real reasons."
- "The 'free gift' in that new launch is why your unit is overpriced."

When to use: education/protection content. High saves and shares — people send it to a friend who's buying.

## 4. The "If… then" Hook (curiosity gap)

You set up a condition that promises a reveal.

**Patterns:**
- "If your household income is under RM5k, this scheme was made for you."
- "If you're paying RM1,800 rent in KL, you can probably own instead. Watch."
- "If you buy a condo without checking the maintenance fee, you'll regret it. Here's why."
- "If you're a first-time buyer, the stamp duty exemption saves you up to RM11k — but only until this date."

When to use: scheme explainers, affordability content, conditional advice.

## 5. The Direct Address (cuts through, high conversion)

You name the audience directly.

**Patterns:**
- "If you're 25–30 and think you can't afford KL — watch this before you give up."
- "First-time buyers, this is the only video you need on stamp duty this year."
- "To everyone renting in Cheras who DM'd me — here's what your money could buy instead."
- "Young couples in KL: your combined income opens up more than you think."

When to use: when you want a specific sub-segment to engage. Lower reach, higher enquiry rate.

## 6. The Area Reveal Hook (high saves, local SEO)

You anchor on a place. KL viewers are hyper-local and will stop for their own neighbourhood.

**Patterns:**
- "What RM500k actually gets you in Old Klang Road vs Cheras vs Setapak."
- "The most underrated area in KL for first-time buyers and nobody's talking about it."
- "Everyone sleeps on this KL neighbourhood. Prices, transit, the honest downsides."
- "Ranking KL areas for your first condo — from 'run away' to 'buy now'."

When to use: area guides. These get saved and reshared to local groups — great organic reach.

## 7. The Question Hook (bait-and-deliver)

You open with a question the viewer wants answered.

**Patterns:**
- "Condo or landed for your first home in KL? The honest answer depends on one thing."
- "Why is a RM450k condo in KL cheaper per month than renting? Most people can't explain it."
- "Rent or buy in 2026? Here's the actual number that decides it — not vibes."
- "What's the real monthly cost of owning a KL condo? It's not just the loan."

When to use: when the question genuinely splits the audience and the answer is non-obvious.

## 8. The Visual Hook (silent-watcher safe)

The hook isn't what you say — it's what's on screen. Every property video needs this in addition to the spoken hook, since most Reels are watched sound-off.

**Patterns:**
- Open on the view from the balcony, big text overlay: "RM420K. KL. NO WAY."
- Open on a Maps zoom to the MRT station, text: "6 MIN TO MRT"
- Open on the loan-repayment number filling in on screen: "RM1,850/MONTH"
- Open mid-walkthrough on the best feature (the kitchen, the view), text: "WAIT FOR THE PRICE"

When to use: every video. Lead with the most striking frame — the view, the price, or the transit pin.

## Hook anti-patterns (don't do these)

- "Hi guys, welcome back to my channel..." → Death.
- "So today I want to talk about property..." → Death.
- "Nestled in a vibrant enclave of modern living..." → Developer-brochure death.
- "Let me introduce you to this beautiful unit..." → Death.
- "In this video I'll show you..." → Death.

The pattern: anything that delays the actual content — or sounds like a brochure — is a hook killer. Lead with the number, the view, or the contrarian take.

## Quick test: is your hook good?

Read the first sentence out loud. Ask:
1. Does it create tension, surprise, or curiosity — or show a striking number/frame?
2. Would I scroll past it?
3. Does it make a specific promise about what's coming?

If "yes / no / yes" — ship it. Otherwise, rewrite.
