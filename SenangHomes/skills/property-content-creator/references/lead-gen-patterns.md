# Lead-Gen / Conversion Patterns — Property

How to turn viewers into **booked viewings and WhatsApp/DM enquiries** without sounding like every hard-selling agent on PropertyTok.

## The core principle

The hard truth: **direct "DM me to buy this unit!" content does not convert and tanks your account.** It gets few comments, the algorithm reads it as an ad, and it teaches your audience that you're a salesperson rather than someone who's genuinely on their side.

What works instead is **value-first content where booking a viewing or asking a question is the natural answer to a problem you've already established.** The viewer should feel like you're solving their problem, not closing them.

For Senang Homes, the brand promise is making buying feel *senang* (easy). Every lead-gen video should lower a buyer's confusion or fear by one notch — the enquiry is what happens *because* you helped, not because you pushed.

## The 4-pattern playbook

### Pattern 1: The "here's exactly what you get" walkthrough

You show a real unit honestly — including one downside — so the viewer trusts you. Trust is the conversion.

**Structure:**
- Hook: a specific number ("RM430k, 6 minutes to MRT, here's the honest tour.")
- Tour: the good — layout, view, facilities, the transit link
- Honesty: **one real drawback** ("Maintenance is RM380/month, that's on the higher side — worth knowing.")
- The read: who this unit is genuinely right for ("If you're a couple who commutes to KL Sentral, this makes sense.")
- Soft CTA: "Want the full floor plan and the actual numbers? Drop a 🏠 or DM — no pressure."

**Why it works:** The one honest downside makes everything else believable. People enquire because they trust you, not because you hid the flaws.

### Pattern 2: The "what to check before you buy" educational video

Pure value. You teach the audience how to evaluate a unit/area/loan. Your expertise is the product; the enquiry is the by-product.

**Structure:**
- Hook: "Most first-time buyers in KL skip this check and overpay by RM30k."
- The criteria: 3–4 things that actually matter (transacted vs asking price, maintenance fee, loan margin, transit access)
- The example: "Here's how I'd check it for a unit like this."
- Soft CTA: "If you want me to run these checks on a place you're eyeing, that's what the DMs are for."

**Why it works:** Even viewers who don't enquire walk away smarter — educational content gets saved and shared, which boosts the algorithm and builds you as the trusted voice they come to *when* they're ready.

### Pattern 3: The "can you actually afford it" reality-check video

You show the real maths — repayment, income needed, schemes that apply. High intent, high trust.

**Structure:**
- Hook: "A RM450k condo needs less salary than you think. Let's do the real maths."
- Show: down payment, monthly repayment at current rates, the stamp-duty exemption, which scheme fits
- Reality: the honest total monthly commitment (loan + maintenance + sinking fund)
- Soft CTA: "Want me to run these numbers for your budget? Tell me your target price in the comments or DM."

**Why it works:** Affordability is the #1 fear. Removing it is the highest-trust thing you can do. Enquiries from this content are warm and ready.

### Pattern 4: The "budget challenge" series

A repeatable format. "What does RM[X] buy in KL right now?" — different budget or area each week.

**Structure:**
- Hook: "RM400k in KL — budget challenge, episode 7."
- Show: 2–3 real options at that budget, honest pros/cons
- Insight: one takeaway about what that budget realistically gets you now
- CTA: "New budget every week — comment your number and I'll do it next. DM if you want the actual listings."

**Why it works:** Series content trains the algorithm to push to the same buyer audience repeatedly, and each episode is a soft lead magnet — the "DM for listings" is wanted, not pushed.

## Conversion CTA hierarchy (least to most direct)

Use the softest one that still gets the enquiry.

1. **Implicit:** "This kind of unit exists in KL right now." (No CTA — the intrigued viewer goes to your profile/DMs.)
2. **Comment prompt:** "Comment your budget and I'll tell you what it buys." (Low pressure, high engagement — comments boost reach.)
3. **Emoji/DM opt-in:** "Drop a 🏠 and I'll send the full breakdown." (Warm, opt-in, feels helpful.)
4. **Direct-but-warm:** "If you're serious about viewing, DM me — I'm a real person, no spam, no pressure. I get a commission only if you actually buy, so I'd rather find you the right place than any place." (Honest, transparent. KL audiences reward this.)

**Avoid:**
- "DM ME NOW before it's GONE!!"
- "Only 2 units left, don't miss out!!"
- "Serious buyers only, WhatsApp me 📲📲📲"

These attract low-intent tyre-kickers and signal hard-sell-agent energy the audience distrusts.

## Compliance / honesty notes

- **Disclose you're the agent.** A brief "I'm the agent on this, just being upfront" builds more trust than it costs. Modern buyers assume it anyway.
- **Don't promise price growth.** "This will appreciate / double / go up" is not something you can guarantee — it's credibility poison. You can cite a *specific* demand driver ("an MRT station opening here in [year]") without promising returns.
- **Don't quote scheme figures you haven't verified.** Stamp-duty thresholds and scheme eligibility change with every Budget — a wrong number in a video costs a viewer real money and costs you trust.
- **Don't badmouth other agents/developers by name.** Bad look, sometimes legally risky. Compare features honestly without naming.
- **Protect client/owner privacy.** Don't put exact unit numbers, full addresses, or an owner's personal situation on a public Reel without consent.

## Cadence

Lead-gen / listing content should be **roughly 1 in every 4–5 posts.** If half your videos are unit pitches, your account becomes a listings feed and both the algorithm and the audience turn on you. The other 3–4 videos are pure value (area guides, scheme explainers, affordability maths, myth-busting, mindset). The non-pitch content is what builds the trusting audience that the lead-gen videos then convert.

## The pin comment trick

On any lead-gen/listing post, your **first comment** (pinned) should NOT be "DM me." It should be a question or extra value that surfaces buyer intent:

- "What's your target budget for your first place — 300k, 400k, or 500k+? I'll do a video for the most-voted."
- "Want the loan repayment breakdown for this one? Say the word 👇"

The enquiry path (DM/WhatsApp/bio) is always available. The pin comment exists to drive comment engagement — which boosts algorithmic reach — while quietly qualifying who's actually ready to buy.
